Highlights

A $100 carpet that leaked 1 BTC. A community member bought a carpet for bitcoin on Facebook Marketplace and looked the on-chain payment up on a block explorer. It showed the seller’s wallet held a full bitcoin. Now a stranger knew what the seller looked like, where they lived and how much they had. That is what a privacy leak looks like in real life.

Key takeaways

1. “Nothing to hide” is the wrong question. Privacy is about the balance of power between you and whoever holds your data. Laws change, but data stays: Executive Order 6102 made owning gold illegal in the US overnight. Think of Kafka’s The Trial: the danger is them knowing everything about you while you know nothing about what they hold.

“Arguing that you don’t care about the right to privacy because you have nothing to hide is no different than saying you don’t care about free speech because you have nothing to say.”

Edward Snowden, quoted on the call

2. Bitcoin’s base layer is transparent on purpose. Anyone can verify the 21 million supply. Privacy is added in layers on top.
