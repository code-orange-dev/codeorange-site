Highlights

1Why privacy

Why a Privacy Track at all? You wouldn’t post your bank statements online. On Bitcoin, anyone can read your payments and your balance unless you take care. The track opened with three reasons privacy matters.

Why we need privacy

01 SovereigntyNobody needs to know where you buy coffee, what you buy online, or how much you hold.
02 No censorshipYou shouldn’t need permission to pay someone. Privacy makes censorship harder.
03 FungibilityOne bitcoin is one bitcoin. No “clean” and “tainted” coins, no judging coins by their history.
04 Trade-offYou can’t get 100% privacy for 0% effort. Every tool asks something of you.

“If we don’t have private payments, Bitcoin as an asset will fail.”

Jeff Booth’s idea, quoted on the call: without private payments it can be captured and centralized like gold

3How PayJoin works

The guesses it breaks. Chain analysis usually assumes two things: every input in a transaction belongs to the same person, and the big leftover output is the sender’s change. PayJoin puts a coin from the receiver into the transaction, so both guesses can be wrong. And unlike a CoinJoin, it looks like any normal payment, so it hides in the crowd.

Why it took years to get right. The obvious design is that the sender asks “I want to pay you X” and the receiver offers a coin. But a sender who asks again and again, without ever paying, learns every coin the receiver owns. This is called UTXO snooping. The real protocol fixes it by making the sender commit first:

v1 vs v2. In v1 the receiver must run a web server open to the internet, which is why phone and desktop wallets struggled to receive. PayJoin v2 (“async PayJoin”) swaps that for two helpers run by others, and also hides the sender’s IP address from the receiver.

The one rule: the relay and the mailbox must be run by different people who don’t collude. Then neither sees the whole picture.

Key takeaways

1. PayJoin is not a sidechain or a fork. It is a way for two wallets to talk, the way email is a protocol. The end result is a normal Bitcoin transaction built in steps with PSBTs.

2. It is not instant privacy. One PayJoin won’t clean up coins that are already linked to you, and it only beats two of the chain-analysis guesses. It works best used again and again, together with other tools. The more people use it, the less chain analysis can trust any guess.

3. Try it yourself. Turn on PayJoin in Cake Wallet’s settings and copy the receive link (it’s long and contains pj=). Paste it into Bull Bitcoin and send. On the call only amounts over 10,000 sats went through, and not every try worked, so start small and check mempool.space.
