Highlights

1Diego's open lab: agent sessions you can read like a night sky

Observability, made visual. Guest speaker Diego (Argentina, 15 years in IT) built everything he showed with Claude Code. His open lab takes the telemetry a coding agent already produces and draws each session as a galaxy: the orchestrator in the middle, sub-agents around it, tools and hooks firing as lights between them. Colours mean something. A lot of red means too many guardrails are firing and the work is stuck. There is a second version for LangGraph.

2A cyborg company: agents at desks, humans accountable

A company you can walk through. Diego's startup shows a business as 3D buildings with departments inside. Each desk has an agent with a face and a job. You can see what it is doing, talk to it by voice, and read reports by department. The goal is to let non-technical people, and people with disabilities, work with agents.

3An agent pays for data over Lightning

Real sats, live. keypleb vibe coded a small demo before the call. The agent's job: write a live Bitcoin network briefing. Key takeaways

1. Agents can already hold and spend sats. Paying per call fits how agents work: model calls, data feeds, web search, compute, paywalled content, even a tiny fee to keep spam away.

2. Keep a human accountable. Budget caps, hooks that block broken rules and a signature for anything that matters. The more an agent can do, the more this counts.

3. Show your work to win work. Diego's lab is his portfolio. keypleb rebuilt the website, a dashboard and the ads for a small business with an agent, and that story is now the pitch for the next client.
