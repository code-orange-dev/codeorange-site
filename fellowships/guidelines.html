This is not a course. You're an open-source contributor working on a real project, with structure around you: a mentor, a weekly rhythm, a peer group of fellows, and a dashboard that turns your work into a public track record.

The deal in one line: you show up every week and tell us honestly how it's going; we pay you, unblock you, and help you get to your next step.

Who should apply

The bar to apply

We pick people who have already started. Promise alone isn't enough, but you don't need to be an expert either: we judge where you're heading, not just where you are.

Must have

  • You've done the learning. A Code Orange cohort (like Decoding Bitcoin), Chaincode, Summer of Bitcoin, Btrust, or serious self-study.
  • At least one PR or real review on the project you want to work on. Small is fine; it proves you can navigate the codebase.
  • A working setup: you can build the project and run its tests.
  • Time: 15–20 hours a week for 6 months.
  • A 6-month plan with a goal for each month.
  • Okay with working in public: your monthly highlights and GitHub work go on your proof-of-work page.

Makes you stand out

  • A maintainer or contributor of the project who will vouch for your plan.
  • Work on a Privacy Track project: Payjoin, Silent Payments, Floresta, JoinMarket, OpenSwap, Fedimint, ecash.
  • Reviews of other people's PRs, not just your own code.
  • Showing up: Code Review Club, BitDevs, project calls, Discord.
  • Helping others learn: a workshop, a write-up, answering newcomers.
  • Builders from Southeast Asia and from regions with few Bitcoin developers get priority.

Not there yet? Good to know, not a failure. Join Decoding Bitcoin or the Privacy Track, open your first PR, and apply next round. The readiness check tells you exactly what's missing.

What counts as work

PRs, yes, but also code review, tests, benchmarks, bug reports with a reproduction, docs, and design discussion. A careful review on someone else's PR is worth as much as a small PR of your own. Merges are slow in Bitcoin: we never judge a month by merge count alone, and we never want low-value PRs opened just to look busy.

Milestones

The 6-month arc

You set one milestone per month with your mentor in month 1. These are the defaults; your plan can differ.

  1. Month 1 · Get set upBuild and tests pass, introduce yourself to the project, read recent PRs to learn its habits, open your first (small) PR, and refine your 6-month plan with your mentor.
  2. Month 2 · Find your grooveTwo or more PRs, at least one with real functionality (not just typos). Review others' work every week.
  3. Month 3 · Check in honestlyKeep shipping. Month-3 review with your mentor: what's working, what to change, is the project still the right fit?
  4. Month 4 · Go deepTake on something substantial: a feature, a refactor, a hard bug. Start being a peer, not a newcomer.
  5. Month 5 · Finish the big pieceLand it, or get it close. Start your handoff: what's next after the fellowship?
  6. Month 6 · Land it and show itWrap up open PRs, document your work so others can pick it up, demo it to the community, and apply for your next step: long-term open-source support, a role, or an extension.

Money, clearly

Getting paid

  • $512 a month, paid in bitcoin. The dollar amount is fixed; the number of sats depends on the price.
  • The price: the Coinbase BTC-USD spot price at the moment we send the payment. We never change this mid-fellowship.
  • When: send your monthly report by the 5th; once it's approved, you're paid by the 10th.
  • How: your choice of Lightning, on-chain or ecash, and you can switch any month. Add it to your report.
  • Safety: if you change your payout address, we confirm it with you on a second channel first, so a hacked account can never redirect your pay.
  • Private: payments, amounts and addresses are never shown publicly. Only you and the organisers see them on your dashboard.
  • Taxes: this is a fellowship, not a job. Any tax where you live is yours to handle.

If anything about money is ever unclear, ask right away. We'd much rather over-explain.

Accountability with support

When a month goes sideways

Open source is messy: builds break, reviews stall, life happens. A fellow who reports "nothing merged, here's what I was stuck on and what I'm trying next" is in good standing. A fellow who goes quiet for three weeks isn't, because we can't help someone we can't see.

GreenStandups and reports coming in. Carry on.
YellowMissed standups or a stuck project. We call you and agree a 30-day recovery plan together.
RedNo contact or no progress after the recovery plan. We pause or end the fellowship, with a respectful handoff.

What happens, step by step

  1. Miss a Monday standup: a friendly ping.
  2. Two weeks without a word: a call with your mentor or the organisers.
  3. A month off track: we name the concern, talk, and write a 30-day plan together.
  4. Still off track: we pause or end the fellowship. The last month you worked is always paid.

Pausing

You can pause for up to 2 months, no explanation needed. There's no pay during a pause, and the fellowship gets longer by the same amount. Pausing is always better than disappearing.

Reports late? Tell us before the 5th and it's a non-event. Being honest about a bad month never counts against you; hiding one is the only thing that does.

Your track record

Public proof of work

Every fellow gets a public page on codeorange.dev that shows their work as it happens: the PRs they open, the reviews they write, their milestones and the highlights of each monthly report. It's your portfolio for maintainers, long-term funding programs and employers, and it builds itself as you work.

PublicPrivate (you + organisers only)
Your name or nym and GitHub · your project · your 6 milestones · PRs you opened and reviewed on GitHub · monthly highlights (summary, PRs, reviews, what's next) · how many weeks you posted a standup Your application · weekly standups · blockers and help requests · payments, amounts and addresses · your email

If being public about Bitcoin work puts you at real personal risk where you live, tell us. We'll keep your page private, no questions asked, and it won't count against you.

Professionalism

How we work

  • Respect maintainers' time. Read the contributing guide, keep PRs small and focused, write good descriptions, answer review comments.
  • Review before you ask for review. Reviewing others is how you learn a codebase and earn trust.
  • AI tools are fine; AI slop is not. You must understand and be able to explain every line you submit. Never open a PR you haven't tested and read yourself. Low-effort generated PRs hurt your reputation and the project's.
  • Learn in public. Share what you learn: a thread, a Nostr note, a short write-up or a session at Code Review Club. Once a month is plenty.
  • Pay it forward. Help one newer learner during your fellowship: answer questions in Discord, pair with a Decoding Bitcoin student, or run one session.
  • Be kind. You represent yourself and Code Orange in public project discussions. Our code of conduct applies everywhere.

Our side of the deal

What you get

  • $512 a month in bitcoin for 6 months, so you can spend your time on open source instead of side gigs.
  • A mentor who contributes to your project or one close to it, matched before you start, with a weekly call.
  • Your fellow dashboard: standups, monthly reports, milestones and payment history in one place.
  • A public proof-of-work page that turns your work into a track record anyone can check.
  • A peer group of fellows, plus Code Review Club, the Privacy Track and the Code Orange community.
  • Help with your next step: intros to maintainers and Bitcoin companies, references for good work, and help applying for long-term open-source support.

The process

How to apply

  1. Take the readiness checkTwo minutes. It tells you if you're ready now or what to do first.
  2. Fill in the applicationYour proof of work, the project, and your 6-month plan with a goal per month.
  3. We read every applicationA human replies within 2 weeks. Shortlisted applicants get a short call on Discord.
  4. Offer and mentor matchWe only start a fellowship once your mentor is confirmed.
  5. Start on the 1stYou get your fellow dashboard, set your milestones and post your first standup.
