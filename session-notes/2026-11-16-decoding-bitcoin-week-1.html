Highlights

1Ten Mondays, one plan

We are here to understand how Bitcoin actually works. So the cohort spends its first five weeks on transactions: how one lives, what it looks like byte by byte, how it gets built, signed and paid for. Then script and multisig, a week to build (or vibe code) your own project, and Taproot to finish, followed by a graduation and certificates.

Every week runs the same loop. Keep it simple, the hosts kept saying. The structure is light even when the topics get deep:

How a week works

MondayExplain and discussYou get called on to explain your question in your own words. Everyone chips in.
AfterPractiseExercises on Decoding Bitcoin and the five-question weekly check on our site.

The certificate is for doing the work. Attendance matters, because we keep each other accountable, so book Mondays in your calendar and say so if you have to miss one. The exercises count too. A slide called them optional; that was a mistake, they are part of the certificate. They can get advanced, so take your time.

Can't come live? You can still earn it. One of the first questions: what about people with other obligations who do the homework and post it on Discord, but can't join the call? The answer was yes, that counts. Go through the course on your own, post your work on Discord and ask your questions there. You can exceed expectations without ever being on the call.

And if you just want to hang out, that's fine too. No certificate without the exercises, but you are welcome every Monday for the discussion. Cameras stay off for privacy.

2Your dashboard, no login

Sign up once, get a personal link. Twenty-five people signed up before the first call. Signing up drops you straight into your own dashboard: this week’s lessons, your assigned question, the exercises to tick off, and the weekly check, five quick questions whose results land back on your dashboard.

Why there is no password. The first version used a Nostr login, and it added friction. So now your dashboard is a personal URL. Save it somewhere, like your email or notes, because that link is your key. No accounts to manage, minimal friction.

Built with AI, shaped by you. The whole dashboard was vibe coded with Claude, and it is brand new, so things will change. Every bit of feedback goes straight back into the next version.

3One letter, half the hash

The best moment of the call came from the Decoding Bitcoin quiz. Alex shared his screen and worked through the quiz live: everything green until one question about what happens to a hash when you change a single letter. The intuitive answer is “only the last few characters change”. The real answer: roughly half of all the bits flip. That is the avalanche effect, and it is why a hash gives away nothing about how close two inputs are. Try it yourself:

    5What comes after: the BOSS Challenge

    Decoding Bitcoin is a gentle start. BOSS is the deep end. The Bitcoin Open Source Software Challenge from Chaincode Labs is a bootcamp that wants to turn you into a Bitcoin open source contributor as fast as possible, and another round is coming next year. Razor went through it, and described it like this:

    BOSS in three months

    ChallengesMonth 1
    →
    Your own projectMonth 2
    →
    Contribute to a real projectMonth 3
    →
    Keep goingNo end

    Month one is build-a-transaction, build-Taproot, spend-it style challenges. “There’s no end to the challenge, actually.” (Razor)

    It is a lot of work. Alex joined BOSS in January and did not finish: it was like a full-time job, five to ten hours a day in the first month, next to other obligations. Razor finished, and less than a year after first getting curious about Bitcoin he is now a full-time developer and a Code Orange educator fellow. Dropout rates are high everywhere: our last study cohort, rawBit, had 20 sign-ups and two finishers. That is fine. A handful of graduates is a success.

    6Next Monday: you teach

    Everyone on the call gets a question. Week 1 has six discussion questions. Each person gets one, tagged on Discord and, new idea from the call, by email too (as long as you signed up on the site). Next Monday you get called on and have a few minutes to explain it. If you get question 4, you will have to really understand the UTXO model and put it in your own words.

    “The teacher learns the most.”

    The idea behind the weekly discussion

    Week 1 discussion questions

    Q1What problem did Bitcoin solve that DigiCash, b-money and Bit Gold could not?
    Q2Pick one moment from the Bitcoin History lesson. Why does it matter today?
    Q3What is a hash function? Three properties Bitcoin relies on, and where each shows up.
    Q4Explain the UTXO model in your own words. Why is no balance stored anywhere?
    Q5One advantage and one disadvantage of UTXOs compared with accounts.
    Q6Why “don’t trust, verify”? What can you verify yourself?

    Where the exercises live. Alex and keypleb agreed the plan on the call: the discussion questions and assignments run on the Code Orange site and Discord, and for exercises the cohort uses Decoding Bitcoin, built by The Bitcoin Dev Project. “The Decoding Bitcoin website is the truth”: tested, solid, and if you are already confident with the questions, push through all its exercises.

    Key takeaways

    • Keep it simple. One question, one Monday, one set of exercises at a time. The topics get deep, the structure stays light.
    • Showing up is the skill. Dropout is normal in every cohort. Live or async through Discord, the people who keep coming are the ones who finish.
    • Remember why you are here. A Bitcoin job, a fellowship, your own project, a co-founder on the call, or just understanding how it works. All valid. Meet people, DM each other, make friends.
