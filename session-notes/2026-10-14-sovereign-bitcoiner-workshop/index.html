TL;DR

  • “Nothing to hide” is the wrong question. Privacy is about power.
  • Bitcoin is public on purpose. Privacy is added in layers on top.
  • Custodial wallets can hand your payment history to anyone who asks.
  • Pick your defences with a simple threat model, not fear.

Highlights

A $100 carpet that leaked 1 BTC. A community member bought a carpet for bitcoin on Facebook Marketplace and looked the on-chain payment up on a block explorer. It showed the seller’s wallet held a full bitcoin. Now a stranger knew what the seller looked like, where they lived and how much they had. That is what a privacy leak looks like in real life.

A live Lightning privacy test. We passed sats between four wallets on the call, then traced what each company could hand over if a government came knocking.

If a government asks, each company can hand over:

  • the account behind each payment (email, phone)
  • amounts, times and the node it went to next
  • possibly your IP address

Do better: a self-custodial wallet like Phoenix, your own node behind Tor, and no email sign-up.

Key takeaways

1. “Nothing to hide” is the wrong question. Privacy is about the balance of power between you and whoever holds your data. Laws change, but data stays: Executive Order 6102 made owning gold illegal in the US overnight. Think of Kafka’s The Trial: the danger is them knowing everything about you while you know nothing about what they hold.

“Arguing that you don’t care about the right to privacy because you have nothing to hide is no different than saying you don’t care about free speech because you have nothing to say.”

Edward Snowden, quoted on the call

2. Bitcoin’s base layer is transparent on purpose. Anyone can verify the 21 million supply. Privacy is added in layers on top.

The big picture

3. Be productively paranoid, but practical. You can’t defend against everyone. Answer four questions, then pick your tools:

Your threat model

01 Who?Who am I protecting my bitcoin from?
02 How likely?How likely is it that they come for it?
03 How bad?What happens if I fail?
04 How much effort?How much time and money will I spend?

Resources shared

Reading

  • The Eternal Value of Privacy, Bruce Schneier (2006)
  • ‘I’ve Got Nothing to Hide’ and Other Misunderstandings of Privacy, Daniel J. Solove (2007)
  • Why Privacy Matters, Privacy Guides
  • A Cypherpunk’s Manifesto, Eric Hughes (1993)
  • The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto, Timothy C. May (1988)
  • Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System, Satoshi Nakamoto (2008)
  • 1984 by George Orwell and The Trial by Franz Kafka, used as the two metaphors for surveillance (self-censorship) and data collection (power imbalance)
  • Executive Order 6102 (1933). Fun fact from the call: Bitcoin’s difficulty adjusts every 2016 blocks, which is 6102 backwards.
  • Intel Management Engine, on why a general-purpose laptop is a hard place to keep keys

Tools

  • mempool.space: block explorer used in the carpet story
  • 1ML: Lightning node explorer
  • LN Proxy: wraps a Lightning invoice so payers don’t see your node (used by RoboSats)
  • Fedimint and Fedi: federated ecash mints
  • White Noise and Keychat: private messengers built on Nostr, Keychat with ecash and Lightning built in
  • Wallets mentioned: Phoenix, Blink, Primal, Wallet of Satoshi, BlueWallet (can freeze UTXOs), Sparrow
